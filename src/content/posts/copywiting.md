## Copywriting Advice by Claude

- The best idea from your own material is missing. Your strongest emotional proof was the client saying “I don’t have to worry about anything anymore”.
- The page shows the stakes (what goes wrong) but never the success picture (what life looks like afterwards).
- The speed, the replies in hours and the founder’s involvement appear only as FAQ answers, not in the story.
- Fear works best in a short dose with relief right behind it. compress the four traps into one tight section, with the story as the emotional peak. Follow it immediately with the “worry-free” outcome and a named testimonial.
- You have a real result, a site moved from page four back to page one in about two weeks, and it isn’t on the page. The one testimonial praises service but states no outcome. Add the page-four-to-page-one result and two or three screenshots of real sites.
- “Understand, Plan, Build, Keep it moving” is every agency’s process slide. The six-service grid is the service soup you told me to avoid. I kept both because your template had them, but they carry no persuasion. Drop the “pieces together” section and shrink the services grid.
- A web agency’s homepage shows no sites it built and no face. The founder’s involvement is your trust asset, and the page never says who the founder is. People trust people, and the stock “team around a table” photos work against the authenticity you’re selling.
- Add a face and name near the top.
- “Tell us your idea. Get a straight answer in 4–8 hours.” so a “typical projects start at…” line is worth considering,
